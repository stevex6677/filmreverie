The catalog describes nominal two-inch legend repetition. Our 45.5mm top-track cadence and 60.67mm lower-track cadence are an approximation of the photographed dual numbering layout across a 728mm usable span. The legend is placed between upper numbers to keep it legible. Numbering follows film distance across display cuts rather than being assigned anew to each 6×4.5, 6×6, 6×7 or 6×9 exposure. Rolls longer than that modeled span repeat the factory tracks; UI frame numbers remain the actual imported sequence.

Current stock identities are checked against Kodak's [E100](https://www.kodakprofessional.com/photographers/film/color/kodak-professional-ektachrome-film-e100/531), [Portra 160](https://www.kodakprofessional.com/photographers/film/color/kodak-professional-portra-160-film/529), and [Portra 800](https://kodakprofessional.com/photographers/film/color/kodak-professional-portra-800-film/528) specifications and the existing M9 stock references. E100 uses KODAK E100 and stays positive-only. We do not invent batch codes, barcode bits, emulsion-generation suffixes, or camera exposure-data imprints.

The direct Ektar photograph provides the strongest visible geometry/marker evidence. The same Kodak track arrangement is used for the other supported 120 stocks, with their own names and base colors; individual current production rolls were not verified for every stock. Font shape (bold sans-serif at about 1.3mm), registration, end-cut position, and base color remain approximations. No film grain, camera-specific ragged gate, scratches, or damage is fabricated. The 61mm film / 56mm gate gives 2.5mm outer rails.

Additional inspected downloads from [Tim Anderson / Negative Supply](https://www.negative.supply/blog/2021/3/8/negative-supply-spotlight-tim-anderson) and [Lina Bessonova](https://www.linabessonova.photography/airport-scanners/) cropped off the physical borders. They were excluded from border reconstruction. Exaframe's full-border image host could not be downloaded; it is not claimed as inspected evidence.

The user's review explicitly supersedes M9's photo-only positive preview and M12's fit-with-padding behavior. Positive mode now reverses the complete negative strip after orange-mask normalization: the base becomes dark and its dark edge print becomes light. This is a viewing conversion; a physical negative does not itself change. The photo opening is always center-cropped without an added outline or padding. The original uploaded Blob is retained unchanged.
